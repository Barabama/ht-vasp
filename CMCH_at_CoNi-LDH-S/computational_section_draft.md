# Computational Methods and Results for CMCH@CoNi-LDH-S

## Computational Methods

### Density Functional Theory Parameters

All spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP, version 6.4.3) with the projector augmented wave (PAW) method. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was employed for electron exchange-correlation interactions. To account for the strong on-site Coulomb repulsion of the transition metal 3d electrons, the DFT+U method was applied following Dudarev's formulation, with effective U values of 3.32 eV for Co, 5.00 eV for Mn, and 6.20 eV for Ni. Van der Waals interactions were described using Grimme's DFT-D3 correction (IVDW = 12) to properly capture the interlayer bonding in the layered double hydroxide (LDH) structure.

A plane-wave energy cutoff of 520 eV was used throughout all calculations. The Brillouin zone was sampled using Monkhorst-Pack k-point meshes with a reciprocal density of at least 120 Å³ per k-point for relaxation calculations and 150 Å³ for static calculations. The convergence criteria were set to 10⁻⁶ eV for electronic self-consistency and 0.05 eV Å⁻¹ for ionic forces. Gaussian smearing with SIGMA = 0.05 eV was employed for relaxation, while the tetrahedron method with Blöchl corrections (ISMEAR = -5) was applied for static and density-of-states (DOS) calculations.

All calculations were performed on GPU-accelerated nodes using the VASP GPU port, with KPAR = 4 and NCORE = 2 for relaxation stages, and KPAR = 2 with NCORE = 4 for non-self-consistent (NSCF) band and DOS calculations. Dipole correction (LDIPOL = TRUE, IDIPOL = 3) was applied along the surface normal direction for all slab and heterojunction calculations to eliminate artificial electrostatic fields arising from asymmetric surface terminations.

### Model Construction Strategy

The simulation workflow comprised four hierarchical levels: bulk optimization, surface slab construction, slab relaxation, and heterojunction assembly. The LDH component was modeled as Co₆Ni₆(OH)₁₂ in a 3 × 2 × 2 orthorhombic supercell (90 atoms, a = 9.47 Å, b = 10.91 Å, c = 9.03 Å). To identify the preferential sulfur substitution site, five distinct doping configurations were constructed and compared: four SH⁻-type substitutions with varying Co/Ni coordination environments (3Co, 3Ni, Co1Ni2, Co2Ni1) and one S²⁻-type substitution without hydrogen, each containing one sulfur atom replacing two hydroxyl groups. The results of this screening are discussed in Section 3.1.

The CMCH substrate was derived from the Co₂(OH)₂CO₃ structure (mp-1202876), which crystallizes in the P2₁/c space group (a = 3.20 Å, b = 12.31 Å, c = 9.67 Å). Given that the structure contains equal proportions of tetrahedral and octahedral Co sites, and that Mn²⁺ preferentially occupies octahedral positions in carbonate systems, the four octahedral Co atoms were substituted by Mn to yield CoMn(CO₃)(OH)₂. This substitution pattern (Co₄Mn₄) is consistent with the equimolar Co:Mn ratio employed in the experimental synthesis.

Surface slabs were constructed by cleaving the optimized bulk structures along the (001) plane for LDH and the (010) plane for CMCH, with a vacuum region of approximately 15 Å along the c-axis. The slab thickness was maintained at approximately 4-6 Å (60 atoms per slab), with the bottom third of atoms held fixed during relaxation to mimic semi-infinite bulk conditions. Heterojunctions were assembled using pymatgen's Interface.from_slabs method with an initial surface-to-surface gap of 2.0 Å. To accommodate the lattice mismatch between LDH(001) (10.91 × 9.47 Å²) and CMCH(010) (9.74 × 9.45 Å²), an average in-plane lattice of a = 10.33 Å and b = 9.46 Å was adopted, imposing approximately 5% biaxial strain on each component.

Three heterojunction models were constructed, differing only in the position of the sulfur dopant within the LDH layer: (i) intrinsic heterojunction (S-free LDH), (ii) S-doped heterojunction (S located in the LDH interior, distant from the CMCH interface), and (iii) S-exposed heterojunction (S situated near the interface, exposing the S dopant to the CMCH surface). In all heterojunction calculations, the upper half of the LDH slab was allowed full relaxation while the bottom half of the CMCH substrate was constrained in the z-direction (TTF constraints) to permit in-plane strain relaxation while maintaining the structural reference.

### Post-Processing Analysis

Electronic structure analysis was performed using pymatgen and atomate2. Densities of states (DOS) were computed via non-self-consistent calculations on uniform k-meshes with a reciprocal density of 200 Å³. Band structures along high-symmetry paths were generated using the Hinuma convention for slab geometries. Bader charge analysis was conducted using the Henkelman et al. algorithm via the BaderAnalysis wrapper in pymatgen, with charge transfer defined as Δq = ZVAL − ρ_Bader, where positive values indicate electron loss. Work functions were extracted from the planar-averaged electrostatic potential (LOCPOT) using pymatgen's WorkFunctionAnalyzer, which identifies the vacuum level as the maximum of the electrostatic potential in the vacuum region and computes Φ = V_vacuum − E_Fermi.

## Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Identification of the Sulfur Doping Configuration

To establish a reliable atomic-scale model for sulfur incorporation into the LDH host lattice, six structural configurations were systematically compared: one pristine (S-free) LDH supercell and five distinct S-doped variants encompassing both SH⁻ (protonated) and S²⁻ (deprotonated) substitution forms. The relative thermodynamic stability was assessed through computed substitution energies relative to the pristine structure, incorporating chemical potential corrections derived from molecular reference states (½ H₂, ½ O₂, ⅛ S₈).

Among the SH⁻-type configurations, the binding energy varied modestly with the Co/Ni coordination environment, with the Co₃-terminated configuration exhibiting the lowest formation energy (+1.63 eV), followed by Co₁Ni₂ (+1.63 eV), Co₂Ni₁ (+1.65 eV), and Ni₃ (+1.72 eV). This trend indicates a slight energetic preference for sulfur to coordinate with cobalt over nickel in the LDH host lattice, consistent with the stronger Co-S bond covalency relative to Ni-S. The S²⁻ (deprotonated) configuration showed a higher formation energy (+2.03 eV), which is thermodynamically less favorable than the SH⁻ forms in vacuum. However, the experimental XPS spectrum clearly demonstrates S 2p binding energies at 161.8 and 163.0 eV, which are characteristic of metal-sulfur (M-S) bonds rather than S-H bonds. Under the alkaline hydrothermal conditions employed in synthesis (120°C, pH > 7), the SH⁻ species readily undergoes deprotonation, and within the LDH interlayer, S²⁻ stabilizes through the formation of bridging M-S-M configurations. Therefore, the deprotonated S²⁻ model (CoNiOH2S-noH) was adopted as the representative doping configuration for all subsequent slab and heterojunction calculations.

### 3.2. Bulk Electronic Structure and the Effect of Sulfur Doping

The electronic band structures and projected densities of states (PDOS) were computed for the three bulk systems: pristine CoNi-LDH, S-doped CoNi-LDH, and the CMCH substrate. Pristine CoNi-LDH exhibits a semiconducting band gap of 2.93 eV, with the valence band maximum dominated by Co-3d and Ni-3d states and the conduction band primarily derived from metal d-orbital contributions. The CMCH substrate displays a direct band gap of 2.15 eV, characteristic of transition metal carbonate hydroxide semiconductors.

Upon sulfur incorporation, a dramatic band gap collapse is observed, from 2.93 eV in pristine LDH to just 0.08 eV in S-doped LDH. Examination of the PDOS reveals that this gap narrowing originates from the introduction of S-3p states within the forbidden gap of the host LDH. These S-3p orbitals hybridize with the neighboring Co-3d and Ni-3d states, forming mid-gap impurity bands that effectively bridge the valence and conduction bands. The S-3p partial density of states at the Fermi level reaches 1.65 states eV⁻¹, confirming the formation of a defect-induced metallic-like state. This electronic structure modulation provides a direct DFT-level explanation for the experimentally observed reduction in charge transfer resistance (Rct from 0.78 to 0.57 Ω), as the introduction of mid-gap states significantly enhances the carrier density and facilitates charge transport across the electrode.

The Co d-band center shifts from −0.57 eV in pristine LDH to −0.92 eV in S-doped LDH, indicating a downward shift away from the Fermi level. This shift reflects the charge redistribution induced by sulfur substitution: due to the lower electronegativity of sulfur (2.58) compared to oxygen (3.44), replacing OH⁻ with S²⁻ modifies the local electronic environment around the transition metal centers. The Ni d-band center similarly shifts from −2.69 to −2.96 eV. These d-band center variations are directly linked to the adsorption and charge transfer properties at the electrode-electrolyte interface and are consistent with the enhanced electrochemical performance observed experimentally.

### 3.3. Bader Charge Analysis

Bader charge analysis was performed on all bulk, slab, and heterojunction systems to quantify the charge redistribution associated with sulfur doping and heterojunction formation. The analysis reveals that sulfur in the LDH lattice exhibits a charge transfer of Δq_S ≈ +0.74 to +0.81 e (positive values indicate electron loss), indicating that sulfur adopts a net positive charge state upon substituting the hydroxyl group. This observation is consistent with the electronegativity argument: sulfur (EN = 2.58) is less electronegative than oxygen (EN = 3.44), therefore when S²⁻ replaces OH⁻, the electron density shifts from sulfur toward the neighboring transition metals.

The cobalt and nickel centers in the pristine LDH show negative charge transfers (Δq_Co ≈ −1.31 e, Δq_Ni ≈ −1.26 e), indicating that they gain electron density from the surrounding oxygen ligands. Upon sulfur doping, a small but systematic increase in charge transfer is observed for both metals (ΔΔq_Co ≈ +0.01 to +0.02 e), consistent with a slight reduction in electron density at the metal sites. This trend aligns with the experimental XPS observations, where the Co 2p and Ni 2p binding energies shift by 0.1-0.3 eV toward higher binding energies upon sulfur incorporation, signifying an increased oxidation state of the metal centers.

In the heterojunction systems, the CMCH substrate exhibits distinctive charge transfer characteristics: cobalt atoms near the interface with the LDH show systematically different Bader charges compared to those in the bulk-like region of the substrate, indicating interfacial charge redistribution upon heterojunction formation. The magnitude of this interfacial charge transfer varies with the position of the sulfur dopant, suggesting that the sulfur-induced electronic perturbation propagates to the interface region and modulates the charge distribution across the CMCH-LDH boundary.

The oxygen atoms in the LDH structure show consistently positive charge transfers (Δq_O ≈ +1.19 e), confirming the well-established O²⁻ → Co/Ni charge donation in hydroxyl groups. Hydrogen atoms exhibit negative charge transfers (Δq_H ≈ −0.55 e), consistent with their role as electron acceptors within the hydroxide framework. The total charge balance across all systems sums to zero within numerical accuracy, validating the consistency of the Bader partitioning scheme.
